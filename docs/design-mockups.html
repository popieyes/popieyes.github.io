Three ways in,
one source of truth

The current site has one mode, and the theme is doing the talking. These three shells read from a single typed content layer — so the personality becomes a choice the visitor makes, not a tax they pay. Everything below is a live HTML comp, not a picture of one.

The shared spine

src/content/{profile,experience,projects}.ts holds every fact once. Each mode is a renderer over that data. Adding a project is appending one object — it appears in all three modes, with a real URL at /projects/<slug>. Theme flavour (case IDs, statuses) lives in an optional dossier block that Standard and Spatial simply ignore.

Mode 01 Default landing · indexed · zero JS to first paint

Standard

Built for the person who has eight tabs open and four minutes. Name, role, research, and a way to contact you are all above the fold. Academic serif over a warm off-white — closer to a well-set paper than a product page, because the audience is a research group.

Research

MSLab · 2025 —

My work at MSLab centres on a full pipeline for garment patterns: generating the 2D pattern pieces, assembling them into a wearable topology, and simulating the resulting cloth in real time.

The front end is a custom React / Three.js engine; the simulation and tooling sit behind it. Much of the engineering is in making the two halves agree about geometry.

A second thread covers vectorization of 3D scenes to SVG — turning rendered geometry into resolution-independent output, which is a surprisingly deep visibility problem.

Both threads share a bias: I care about the part of graphics where correctness and performance stop being separable concerns.

RebuildSix directions for the 3D experience

What the spatial mode could be

Ranked by what they actually prove to someone deciding whether to interview you. The old office scene cost 85 MB and demonstrated that you can load a model; these cost less and demonstrate that you can build a renderer.

01
◆ Recommended

The garment table

Your MSLab research, playable. Flat 2D pattern pieces lie on a table; hit assemble and they seam together and fall into a simulated garment. Grab a vertex and drag — the solver responds. Sliders for stiffness, damping, wind.

Why it wins: no other portfolio on earth has this, because it's your actual research. It's the single strongest artefact you could put in front of a graphics lab.

Payoff highEffort high
02
◆ Recommended

Render pass inspector

One scene, and a row of buttons that swap the visible buffer: beauty, albedo, world normal, depth, AO, wireframe. Optionally a split-screen wipe so two passes sit side by side.

Why it wins: it's legible in three seconds to anyone who knows deferred rendering, and cheap — one scene, a handful of shaders. Best payoff per hour on this list.

Payoff highEffort low
03

Live shader editor

A GLSL fragment shader in a panel, its output filling the viewport, recompiling as you type. Preload it with your water shader. Errors surface inline; a reset button undoes the damage.

Trade-off: spectacular for the right visitor, meaningless to a non-technical one. Great as a secondary panel, risky as the whole experience.

Payoff highEffort med
04

Progressive path tracer

Ties to your Nori work. A small scene renders live, accumulating samples — visibly noisy at first, converging over a few seconds, with a sample counter and a denoise toggle.

Trade-off: the convergence is genuinely hypnotic and it self-documents. But it's the heaviest GPU load here, so it needs a hard sample cap and a bail-out.

Payoff medEffort med
05

The workstation, rebuilt

The original concept done properly: a desk where every object maps to a real project. Click the monitor for the engine, the camera for photography, the console for the Unity games. Camera dollies to each hotspot; content is read from projects.ts.

Trade-off: charming, and the models mostly exist. But it proves asset loading, not graphics skill — and decimating office.glb from 31 MB is real work for a modest return.

Payoff medEffort high
06

Performance ladder

A visible quality dial — resolution scale, shadow cascades, SSAO samples — with frame time and draw calls updating live as the visitor degrades their own experience.

Trade-off: a small, cheap addition rather than a mode of its own. Bolt it onto whichever direction you pick; it signals optimisation instinct for almost no cost.

Payoff medEffort low

My read

Ship 02 first — the render pass inspector is a weekend of work and it's the clearest signal on the list. Then build 01, the garment table, as the centrepiece, because it's the one thing that is uniquely yours and it doubles as a live demo of your research. Fold in 06 as HUD chrome around whichever you build.

I'd retire the office scene. It's 85 MB, it's the most work here, and a room with a desk in it says less about you as a graphics programmer than sixty seconds with a depth buffer does. The personality it was carrying now lives in Dossier mode, where it costs nothing.
